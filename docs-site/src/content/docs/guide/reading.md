Skyreader gives you two ways into your reading. **Home** is a calm front page that picks up where you left off. **Feeds** is everything you follow, in one stream, with channels to slice it.

## Home

**Home** gathers what you're in the middle of: pick up where you left off, pieces you asked to [come back to](/guide/your-library/#coming-back-to-a-piece), recent saves, a few things from your Shelf, your daily magazine. It draws on your [Library](/guide/your-library/), and each lane links to where it comes from.

**Continue reading** shows anything you've started and not finished, leaving out what you've archived. The Shelf's own Continue reading still includes a book you archived halfway through. **New connections** appears when someone on Semble connects something to one of your saves, as it does on the Shelf.

Each of your [collections](/guide/your-library/#collections) gets a lane of its own, in the collection's order, with **Open collection** to see the rest. A collection with nothing in it has no lane until you add something.

To change Home, choose **Customize** at the top of it. Pick where the app opens and how large the cards are, hide any section, or drag one by its handle to reorder. Changes apply right away and are saved on this device.

## Feeds

**Feeds** shows everything you follow in one chronological stream. The sidebar lists your sources, and your channels sit together under **Channels**. A **channel** is a saved view over what you read, your feeds, your saves or both, filtered by source or read state, so "Newsletters" or "Slow reads" is one click away.

![The Feeds view: sources and channels in the sidebar, newest articles first in the stream](../../../assets/screenshots/library.png)

### Choosing what shows

Choose **Filter** at the top of the page to sort newest or oldest first, show only unread or everything, and pick sources and tags. On a phone, **View options** in the bottom bar has the same choices, apart from tags. Its **Sources** section lists everything that brings you things to read, grouped:

- **From Bluesky**: **Your likes** and **Your bookmarks** (the links in posts you liked or bookmarked, if you've turned them on), and **People you follow** (the links they share).
- **Newsletters**: every sender, together or one by one.
- **Folders**: each of [your folders](/guide/adding-sources/#folders), whatever's filed in it.
- **Publications**: your feeds and blogs that aren't in a folder, plus **All publications**, which covers every feed and blog, filed or not.

Everything starts checked. Uncheck a source, a folder or a whole group to leave it out. Choose **only** beside one to see just that. **Show everything** puts it all back. A group or folder stays whole as it grows: hide **Newsletters** and a newsletter that arrives tomorrow stays hidden too. On **Feeds**, the filter and the unread/all choice stay put when you come back or reload, on that device. Choose **Save as channel** to keep a filter as a channel.

Items you scroll past can be marked read automatically (a toggle in **Settings → Reading**).

### Making a channel

Choose **New channel** under **Channels**, in the sidebar or the feed switcher on a phone.

- **Name** fills itself in from what you choose. Type your own to keep it.
- **Show** and **Order** set what the channel opens on: unread or everything, newest or oldest first.
- **What's in it**:
  - **Sources I pick**: a new channel starts with every source checked, your Library included; uncheck what it should leave out, or choose **Select none** and check the few you want. Check a folder to take in everything filed there, now and later. Under **From your Library**, **Your saves** takes in everything you've saved, and **Your Shelf** just what's on your Shelf. A save shows by when you saved it, marked **From your Library**, and opens in place like anything else in the channel, in **List** or **Expand**. One you've archived counts as read.
  - **Sources that match**: a rule picks them and keeps up as you follow more. **Daily Digest** takes sources that post several times a day, **Don't Miss** the ones that post rarely, and **Long Reads** the ones that write at length. You can also match the sources you added lately, ones you tagged, or ones from a site.
- **Labels**: leave out posts with a label, such as **Made with AI**, or choose **Only** to keep just the labeled ones. See [Labeled posts](#labeled-posts).
- **Which saves**: a channel of your saves alone, with nothing else checked, opens like your Queue, with **Inbox** and **Archive**. Narrow it by kind, when you saved it, length, site or tag, and order it by length or site too.

To change a channel later, open it and choose **Edit channel**, or right-click it in the sidebar. On a phone, **Edit channel** sits beside **View** in the view options.

### Labeled posts

Some writers label their own posts so you know what you're getting. A post on pckt marked **Made with AI** is the common case, and a post can also be labeled as adult content, nudity or graphic media. The label shows as a small tag beside the post's date. Labels come from blogs on the Atmosphere ([standard.site](/words/) publications such as pckt and Leaflet). RSS feeds don't carry them, and the writer decides whether to add one, so an unlabeled post isn't a promise.

In **Settings → Reading → Content labels**, choose what each label does:

- **Show**: the post appears as usual, with its tag.
- **Warn**: the post appears, but opens to a note naming the label. Choose **Show post** to read it. Adult content, nudity and graphic media start here.
- **Hide**: the post stays out of your feeds and channels, and out of your unread counts. **Made with AI** starts on Show, so pick Hide if you'd rather not see it.

A saved search doesn't apply labels: it shows everything that matches. These settings belong to your account, so they follow you to your other devices, and so does a channel's **Labels** filter. A channel set to **Only** a label you hide in Settings still shows those posts, since that channel asked for them.

### Arranging channels

To change the order of your channels, drag one up or down the sidebar. Or right-click it (on a phone, press and hold) and choose **Move up** or **Move down**. The order follows you to your other devices.

## The reader

Opening an article gives you a clean, full-screen reading surface.

Skyreader preserves the structure of Leaflet and Offprint publications, including galleries,
equations, buttons, task lists, and aligned text. If a publication uses an interactive widget the
reader cannot safely show, the article says so and links you to the original instead of silently
leaving content out. Members-only sections stay on their publication.

![The reader: a full-screen article with quiet chrome and a Discussion section at the end](../../../assets/screenshots/reader.png)

- **Font and size**: change the reading font and the text size from the reader's appearance controls.
- **Paged mode**: a toggle in the reader switches from scrolling to page turns, one page at a time, or two columns side by side on a wide screen. On a phone, swipe left for the next page and right to go back. If you'd rather swipe up and down, open **Style & Actions** (the sliders button at the right end of the bar at the bottom) and, under **Swipe to turn**, choose **Up & down**: swipe up for the next page, down to go back.
- **Contents**: an article with sections, a PDF or a book lists them under **Contents**: beside **Back** on a wide screen, in the bar at the bottom on a phone, or press **c**. The section you're in is marked; choose another to go there. A PDF or a book lists its own table of contents where it has one, in either view of a PDF.
- **Highlights**: once you've highlighted an article, **Highlights** lists every passage, to go back to one or act on several together. See [All of an article's highlights](/guide/saving-and-highlights/#all-of-an-articles-highlights).
- **Footnotes**: a footnote's number shows as a small button. Tap it, or tab to it and press **Enter**, to read the note beside it without losing your place. Tap anywhere else, or press **Esc**, to put it away. A note that points to another note opens that one in the same spot. A link to a section further on still takes you there.
- **Light and dark**: Skyreader follows your device's setting. To keep it light or dark regardless, pick a theme in **Settings → Reading → Appearance**.
- **Highlighting**: double-click a paragraph or select text, then grow it to the whole sentence or paragraph if you like. See [Saving and highlights](/guide/saving-and-highlights/).
- **Images**: click an image to see it full screen. Click again or press **Esc** to close it.

![Paged mode: the same article flowed into two side-by-side columns, turned a page at a time](../../../assets/screenshots/paged-reader.png)

### Reading a few at once: the Desk

Sometimes one article sends you to another, or you're reading two pieces side by side in your head. The **Desk** holds the few things you're reading right now, so you can flip between them without going back to a list. Each one opens where you left off.

- **Put something on the Desk**: in the reader, choose **Desk** (beside **Back** on a wide screen; on a phone, in **Style & Actions**), then **Put this on the Desk**. It holds up to six.
- **Put a link on the Desk**: tap a link in an article and choose **Add to Desk** to line up the page it goes to. If you haven't saved it yet, it's saved to your Queue first.
- **Switch**: open **Desk** and choose another piece. On a phone, once there's something to switch to, a Desk button appears in the bar at the bottom.
- **Back to the last one**: press `` ` `` to flip to the piece you had open before this one, whether or not it's on the Desk. Press it again to flip back. The Desk lists that piece under **Last open** too.
- **Back** still closes the reader, however many times you've switched.

A piece leaves the Desk when you finish it or archive it, or when you take it off with its **×**. The Desk is for one sitting, not for keeping: your [Queue and Shelf](/guide/your-library/) are where things stay, and **Continue reading** on Home lists everything you've started. The Desk is private, and it stays on the device you made it on.

## Discussion

Articles carry a **Discussion** section at the end that gathers what people across the Atmosphere have written about that link: linkblog notes, Leaflet comments, Bluesky posts, Margin annotations, Semble connections, merged into one stream.

### Papers: Cited by

When you're reading a paper, the Discussion also has a **Cited by** tab: the papers and books that cite it, most cited first. Skyreader knows a page is a paper when it has a DOI, the permanent ID most journals and arXiv give a paper, in its link (doi.org, arXiv, bioRxiv, and publisher pages) or in the citation details the page declared when you saved it. The list comes from [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org), an open index of scholarly works, and is refreshed about once a week.

Each entry shows the title, the first few authors, the year, and where it was published. Tap the title to open it (on the publisher's site, or on OpenAlex for a work without a DOI), or the bookmark to save it and read it in Skyreader. One you already saved, in any version, says **In your Library** and opens in the reader. The ten most cited are listed, five at first and the rest under **more**; **See all on OpenAlex** shows every one. Cited by needs you to be signed in. A paper OpenAlex doesn't know yet, or one nobody has cited, has no tab.

To hear when a paper you saved gets new citations, turn on [Watch for updates](/guide/your-library/#watching-for-updates).

### Adding yours

To add yours, use the buttons under **Add yours**. **Bluesky** writes a post from your own account, with the article's link card under your words; it's public like any Bluesky post.

To connect an article to another one on Semble, choose **Connect this to something** (or **Draw the first connection**), then pick how they relate. The other end can be any link: paste one, or search your library.

You don't have to go looking for the article you just finished. At the end of an article, above the Discussion, **Connect this to something you've read** shows your last few reads as cards. Tap one to connect the two, then pick how they relate. The same cards sit under the search field when you open the connect window. They show what you read on this device, plus saves you've read on any device.

To connect the article to a page it links to, tap the link and choose **Connect on Semble…**. Connections are public on Semble.

## The daily magazine

**Daily** builds a magazine issue from your saved articles. Pick an issue length in minutes and an ordering, then **Generate issue**. An issue is a snapshot: new saves don't reshuffle it, and it resumes where you left off on any device. When you finish, archive the issue or generate a new one from the current pile.

![A daily magazine issue: a dated table of contents built from saved articles, followed by the first article](../../../assets/screenshots/daily-magazine.png)

## Offline

Your feeds, saves, and highlights are stored on your device. Reading, marking read, highlighting, and the magazine all work offline; anything you do is queued and synced when you reconnect. Adding sources and changing sync settings need a connection.

In a very large library, Skyreader keeps the article text of your newest and most recently opened saves on your device, so storage doesn't fill up. Every save stays in your list, but an older one you haven't opened lately needs a connection to load its text. Before a trip, open the saves you plan to read and they'll stay with you.
